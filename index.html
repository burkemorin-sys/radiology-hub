Coronary Calcium Score (CAC-DRS)

CategoryAgatstonRiskSCCT treatment recommendation
CAC-DRS 00Very lowStatin generally not recommended
CAC-DRS 11–99MildModerate-intensity statin
CAC-DRS 2100–299ModerateModerate- to high-intensity statin + aspirin 81 mg
CAC-DRS 3>300Moderate to severeHigh-intensity statin + aspirin 81 mg

Notation: CAC-DRS [A|V][0–3]/N[1–4]. A = Agatston score, V = visual estimate (same category cut-offs). N = number of coronary arteries with calcium: left main, LAD, LCx, RCA (N1–N4). Example: CAC-DRS A1/N2. N matters: A1/N4 calls for more aggressive management than A1/N1, even with the same score.

Caveat: SCCT's recommendations were based on the 2013 ACC/AHA prevention guidelines and 2017 SCCT guidance, and apply to adults aged 40–75.

Percentile (MESA)

Express the score as a percentile for age, sex and race/ethnicity (MESA CAC calculator). A score at or above the 75th percentile reclassifies the patient to higher risk. Men <50 and women <60 with any CAC are nearly always above the 75th percentile. Men <60 and women <70 with CAC ≥100 are nearly always above it too.

2018 AHA/ACC cholesterol guideline

Applies to adults 40–75, no diabetes, LDL-C 70–189 mg/dL, 10-year ASCVD risk 7.5–19.9%, when the statin decision is uncertain:

CAC 0Statin may be withheld or delayed, except in smokers, diabetes, or strong family history of premature ASCVD.
CAC 1–99Favors statin, especially at age ≥55. If <75th percentile and the statin is postponed, consider repeat CAC in 5 years.
CAC ≥100 or ≥75th percentileStatin indicated unless deferred after a clinician–patient risk discussion.

Report templates (suggested wording, not official SCCT text)

  • CAC-DRS 0: “No identifiable coronary artery calcium (Agatston score 0). CAC-DRS 0.”
  • CAC-DRS 1: “Mild coronary artery calcium, Agatston score [X], involving [n] vessel(s). CAC-DRS A1/N[n].”
  • CAC-DRS 2: “Moderate coronary artery calcium, Agatston score [X], involving [n] vessel(s). CAC-DRS A2/N[n].”
  • CAC-DRS 3: “Severe coronary artery calcium, Agatston score [X], involving [n] vessel(s). CAC-DRS A3/N[n].”
  • Add if known: “This corresponds to the [P]th percentile for age, sex and race/ethnicity (MESA).”
Source: Hecht et al., SCCT CAC-DRS, J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr 2018;12:185–191 CAC-DRS: A Primer (J Cardiovasc Imaging, free full text, Table 2) Source: Grundy et al., 2018 AHA/ACC Cholesterol Guideline, Circulation Source: 2018 Cholesterol Guideline – CAC/MESA percentile text

Liver Injury Scale (AAST 2018)

GradeImaging criteria (CT)
ISubcapsular hematoma <10% surface area; parenchymal laceration <1 cm depth
IISubcapsular hematoma 10–50% surface area; intraparenchymal hematoma <10 cm diameter; laceration 1–3 cm depth and ≤10 cm length
IIISubcapsular hematoma >50% surface area; ruptured subcapsular or parenchymal hematoma; intraparenchymal hematoma >10 cm; laceration >3 cm depth; any injury with liver vascular injury or active bleeding contained within the liver parenchyma
IVParenchymal disruption of 25–75% of a hepatic lobe; active bleeding extending beyond the liver parenchyma into the peritoneum
VParenchymal disruption >75% of a hepatic lobe; juxtahepatic venous injury (retrohepatic vena cava, central major hepatic veins)

Vascular injury = pseudoaneurysm or AV fistula: focal vascular contrast that decreases in attenuation on delayed images. Active bleeding = focal or diffuse vascular contrast that increases in size or attenuation on the delayed phase. Grade by the highest finding on imaging, at operation or on pathology. Advance one grade for multiple injuries, up to grade III.

Source: Kozar et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2018;85(6):1119–1122 Full text (AAST-hosted PDF) AAST Injury Scoring Scales

Spleen Injury Scale (AAST 2018)

GradeImaging criteria (CT)
ISubcapsular hematoma <10% surface area; parenchymal laceration <1 cm depth; capsular tear
IISubcapsular hematoma 10–50% surface area; intraparenchymal hematoma <5 cm; parenchymal laceration 1–3 cm
IIISubcapsular hematoma >50% surface area; ruptured subcapsular or intraparenchymal hematoma ≥5 cm; parenchymal laceration >3 cm depth
IVAny injury with splenic vascular injury or active bleeding confined within the splenic capsule; laceration involving segmental or hilar vessels producing >25% devascularization
VAny injury with splenic vascular injury and active bleeding extending beyond the spleen into the peritoneum; shattered spleen

Vascular injury = pseudoaneurysm or AV fistula: focal vascular contrast that decreases in attenuation on delayed images. Active bleeding = focal or diffuse vascular contrast that increases in size or attenuation on the delayed phase. Grade by the highest finding on imaging, at operation or on pathology. Advance one grade for multiple injuries, up to grade III.
